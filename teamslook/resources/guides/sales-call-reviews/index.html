How to review a sales call without losing the human story.

A simple review framework for sales leaders who want coaching sessions to lead to better conversations.

A useful sales call review should answer two things: what did the customer need, and how well did the rep help move the conversation forward?

1. Start with the customer's goal.

Before looking at metrics, understand who called, why they engaged and what a successful outcome would have looked like. Context changes the meaning of every number.

2. Review the moments that matter.

  • Opening: was the purpose clear and respectful of time?
  • Discovery: did the rep ask enough to understand the need?
  • Objections: was concern acknowledged and explored rather than dismissed?
  • Next step: was a specific action agreed, with ownership and timing?

3. Give one priority, not ten corrections.

Choose the behavior that most influenced the conversation, show a concrete example, and agree on a practice opportunity for the next week. Good feedback is small enough to act on.

4. Revisit the pattern.

Do the same issues repeat? Is the agent getting better at confirming next steps? Trend lines are useful when paired with real examples, not as substitutes for them.
