Useful things to know

A little clarity goes a long way.

These short field notes explain the decisions that matter before you commit to an expensive tool, campaign or rebuild.

01

Accessibility must be part of the workflow

Readable contrast, keyboard access, correctly labeled inputs, understandable errors and screen-reader compatibility matter throughout design and testing. Applicable requirements vary by jurisdiction and program.

02

Public systems need an audit trail

A useful digital process identifies who can see, approve or change information, and records decisions according to an agreed retention policy. Access controls and records handling should be designed before launch.

03

Start with a service, not a platform

A successful pilot often digitizes one complete public journey, measures demand and friction, then expands carefully. Replacing everything at once introduces avoidable risk.

Questions, answered

The details worth asking about.

Good projects begin with clarity about scope, roles, technical feasibility and what success actually looks like.

Can you help public organizations in the United States?

We can discuss remote web, workflow and digital service projects for US-based public-serving organizations. Any engagement depends on procurement eligibility, contract requirements and relevant laws.

Do you guarantee government accessibility or security compliance?

No. We can design toward agreed standards and support testing, but compliance must be evaluated against the specific jurisdiction, agency policies and formal review requirements.

Can you work with existing legacy databases?

Often, subject to documentation, permitted access and integration feasibility. We start with a review and propose a staged, reversible approach.

Will AI make final public-service decisions?

Our recommended starting point is AI for assisted search, categorization or drafting with appropriate human review—not unsupervised high-impact decisions.

Can you build a small pilot instead of an entire platform?

Yes. A well-defined single-service pilot is often the best way to test usefulness, accessibility and operational fit before broader rollout.
