Listening room / Illustrated example

One conversation. Three things to take away.

Choose a team to see how the same review process answers three different questions: what happened, what to coach, and who needs a callback.

“Before I book the rollout, can you show me how the handoff works?”

Call brief: The buyer wants to see the handoff before choosing a rollout date.

Coaching note: Coach the rep to ask who owns the handoff today. A short discovery question is more useful here than another feature list.

A manager’s field guide

From the morning brief to the last callback.

01 / Start the shift

Read the daily brief

Look for the thread across conversations. If several callers ask about onboarding, start there before reviewing individual calls.

02 / Pick a conversation

Read the summary in context

Use the call brief to identify what the customer asked for, what the agent offered, and what still needs an answer.

03 / Coach one skill

Use the agent scorecard

Choose a moment to discuss together. Did the agent ask a useful follow-up question? Agree on one change to try on the next call.

04 / Close the loop

Review the callback queue

Separate a promised callback from a completed one. Before the next shift, check which conversations still need an owner and a next step.

Demo notebook

Three questions to bring to the demo.

What keeps coming up in sales calls?

Start with Ask your data. Try a narrow question about recurring objections, then compare the answer with individual call summaries before changing your pitch.

Which conversation deserves coaching time?

Read a scorecard alongside a call summary. Pick one observable behavior, such as whether the rep clarified the problem before offering a solution.

Which customers still need an answer?

Review the callback queue and choose a conversation with an open question. Use the summary to prepare a specific reply instead of making the customer repeat the story.
